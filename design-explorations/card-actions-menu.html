Library card actions: five ways to fold them

A finished clip card shows eight buttons today, three of them full-width. Each direction keeps one primary action on the card and moves the rest into a per-card menu. The directions differ in how that menu is triggered, where it sits, and what counts as non-essential.

What works

The card drops from five rows of controls to one. It reads the same in the 4-column dense grid, where "Restore settings" is already squeezed to a bare icon. Section labels keep the last-frame actions grouped the way LastFrameActions groups them today.

What risks

A bare ⋯ is the least discoverable trigger. People who chain clips with "Continue from last frame" lose a visible button they use often. The inline "Copied" confirmation and error line also have to move to toasts.

Pick this if

Continuing from the last frame is a minority path, and you want the convention people already know from Drive, Figma and Linear.

B

Split button

Optimizes for one control that reads as "use this clip ▾", with menu items that say what each one does · Tradeoff it buries Delete and Download inside a "use" control, where they don't belong, and its rich menu is the tallest of the five.

What works

It's the most compact option that still has a clear trigger: the caret sits on the primary itself, so the menu reads as part of the main action. The one-line explanations finally say what "Restore settings" does without a tooltip.

What risks

Split buttons are often misclicked on the wrong half, especially at the 4-column size. The explanations make the menu tall, so it will need to flip upward near the bottom of the library sheet.

Pick this if

You want the card to have exactly one control, and you think people get lost because they don't know what the actions do.

C

Noun menus — "Last frame ▾" · "File ▾"

Optimizes for scent: each text label names what its menu acts on, so you know where to look before you click · Tradeoff two triggers per video card instead of one, and the quiet text labels are easy to skim past.

What works

"Last frame ▾" tells you three actions are waiting inside without listing them, and its items can be shortened ("Save as PNG") because the label already supplies the noun. Image cards simply don't get that label, so the difference between kinds is visible at a glance.

What risks

Muted 13px text is the weakest affordance on a dark card, and "File" is a loose name for something that contains Restore settings. It also adds a row: a video card is one line taller than in A.

Pick this if

You liked the text-label-with-caret idea and want the last-frame actions to stay discoverable as a group without the space they take today.

D

Media overlay toolbar

Optimizes for the quietest card: utilities float on the thumbnail when you hover or focus, and the body keeps one button · Tradeoff nothing hints that actions exist until you hover, and on touch the toolbar must stay visible, covering part of the media.

What works

The grid looks most like a media library (Photos, Frame.io): content first, chrome on demand. Download, the most common utility, stays one click away, and the card is the shortest of the five, so more fits in the two-column sheet.

What risks

The toolbar shares space with VideoPlayer's own controls and its fullscreen corner, which invites misclicks. Hover-only reveal also has to fall back to always-visible on touch screens and when a keyboard user tabs in.

Pick this if

Library browsing is mostly visual scanning, and you want the grid to feel like a gallery, not a control panel.

E

Two forward actions + "More ▾" in the meta line

Optimizes for the generate → continue loop: both ways to send a clip onward stay visible, and the rest sits behind a text caret · Tradeoff it trims the card less than A–D, and the trigger shares a line with metadata that can wrap.

What works

The decision about what's essential is explicit: anything that sends the clip forward stays visible, and anything about the file goes in the menu. Putting "More" on the meta line costs no extra row, and it sits next to the facts about the file, which is what most of its items act on.

What risks

"Continue" alone is ambiguous next to "Add to timeline" (continue what?). Long meta lines (YouTube ranges, "Relaxed", "Temporary", agent names) will compete with the trigger for width in the dense grid.

Pick this if

Chaining clips from the last frame is a core loop for you, and you only want to hide the file housekeeping.

How to pick a direction

The first question is whether "Continue from last frame" is essential. The second is how visible the trigger should be.

A · KebabA familiar convention, and Continue is a minority path.
B · Split buttonOne control per card, with menu items that explain themselves.
C · Noun menusText labels with carets that say what's inside.
D · OverlayA gallery-like grid; actions appear when you need them.
E · Two forwardContinue stays one click; only the file housekeeping is hidden.
